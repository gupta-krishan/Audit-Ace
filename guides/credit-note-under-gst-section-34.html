GST Credit Note: Rules, Mandatory Details and Time Limit

Short answer: a GST credit note is a document a supplier issues to reduce the value or tax on an earlier invoice, for example for returned goods, a post-sale discount or an excess amount charged. Section 34 of the CGST Act governs it.

When a credit note is issued

  • Goods are returned by the buyer.
  • A discount is given after the sale.
  • The invoice value or tax charged was higher than it should have been.
  • The goods or services were found deficient.

Details a credit note must carry

  • Supplier name, address and GSTIN, and the words "Credit Note".
  • A unique serial number and the date.
  • Recipient name, address and GSTIN (if registered).
  • The number and date of the original invoice.
  • The value and tax being credited, and the supplier's signature.

Time limit

A credit note for an invoice of one financial year generally must be issued by 30 November following the end of that year, or the date the annual return is filed, whichever is earlier. For an invoice dated November 2026 (financial year 2026-27), the usual outer date is 30 November 2027.

Effect on input tax credit

The buyer who claimed input tax credit has to reduce it by the tax in the credit note. The supplier can reduce their tax liability only if the conditions in Section 34 are met, so a credit note issued late may not give any tax relief.

Debit notes

A debit note does the opposite: it raises the value or tax of an earlier invoice, for example for a price increase or a shortfall in the tax charged. The 30 November limit applies to credit notes that reduce tax, not to debit notes that increase it.
